## CPG 2.0 and IT/OT convergence: is your infrastructure ready?

On December 11, 2025, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) took a key step forward with the publication of the **Cybersecurity Performance Goals 2.0 (CPG 2.0)**.
After three years of operational analysis and industry feedback, this update turns what used to be recommendations into **measurable, outcome-oriented actions**.

For security leaders in environments where IT and OT converge, this isn't "just another regulation." It's a voluntary but critical roadmap, aligned with **NIST CSF 2.0**, designed to reduce real risks in infrastructure that can't afford downtime.


## From technical to governance: the real shift in CPG 2.0

The big novelty of **CPG 2.0** is the explicit inclusion of the **GOVERN** function.
CISA makes it clear that cybersecurity is no longer just a technical matter, but a **business responsibility**.

**Goal 1.A** requires defining clear roles and, above all, establishing **ongoing collaboration between IT and OT teams**. This breaks down the traditional silos that still exist in sectors such as energy, manufacturing, or transportation.

This vision connects directly with what we've already covered in our article on **[industrial OT systems and networks](/en/blog/sistemas-y-redes-industriales-ot/)**.


## Protection in environments where IT and OT collide

### Asset management and supply chain

**Goal 2.A** of CPG 2.0 insists on maintaining an up-to-date inventory.
While this is usually well controlled in IT, in OT the lack of visibility over **PLCs, HMIs, or legacy devices** remains a structural risk.

In addition, goals **1.D (Supply Chain)** and **1.E (Managed Service Providers)** reinforce the need to evaluate vendors and third parties, a critical point that's also addressed in regulations such as **NIS2**.

You can't protect what you can't see.
**Asset inventory** is the starting point of any industrial cybersecurity strategy.


### Vulnerabilities, patches, and compensating controls

CPG 2.0 acknowledges a common reality in OT: **patching isn't always possible**.
The recent publication of critical advisories affecting **Siemens**, **Johnson Controls**, or industrial toolkits proves it.

When halting an operation isn't viable, CISA validates the use of **compensating controls**, where **network segmentation** and **continuous monitoring** become key barriers.

This approach aligns with the use of advanced detection technologies like the ones we explain in **[NDR in OT environments](/en/blog/ndr-en-ot/)**.


## Detecting before it's too late: visibility and response

**Goals 4.A and 4.B** reinforce the need for **early detection**.
Modern threats, evasive malware, and silent lateral movements make traditional defenses insufficient.

That's why CPG 2.0 connects directly with **24/7 monitoring** models, where services like a **SOC-MDR** allow correlating IT and OT events and distinguishing operational failures from real incidents.

A **SOC** (Security Operations Center) is what makes this model possible: it continuously monitors the network and coordinates incident response.

The difference between a controlled alert and a crisis lies in **governance, visibility, and real detection capability**.
