## What are OT systems and networks

OT systems (Operational Technology) are the systems and technologies used to control physical processes in the real world, such as those found in industrial plants, critical infrastructure, factories, power grids, water treatment, transportation, and more.

OT networks connect sensors, PLCs, and SCADA or DCS systems, which are present across most sectors: energy, transportation, logistics, and many others.

While IT cybersecurity focuses on CONFIDENTIALITY, OT [managed cybersecurity](https://ewala.es/kit-digital-ciberseguridad-gestionada) seeks to guarantee AVAILABILITY, based on physical and logical security in pursuit of continuity of operational processes.

OT devices also use their own communication protocols, specifically designed for industrial environments. These protocols enable real-time data transmission between sensors, controllers, and supervisory systems, with high reliability under demanding operating conditions. Some of the most widely used are Modbus, one of the most widespread protocols, available in variants such as Modbus RTU (serial communication) or Modbus TCP (over IP networks); PROFIBUS and PROFINET, widely used in industrial automation systems, especially with Siemens equipment; BACnet, common in building control systems; DNP3, used in electrical infrastructure; and OPC-UA, which enables more standardized and interoperable communication. However, many of these protocols were designed without modern cybersecurity requirements in mind, making them a risk vector in connected OT environments.

## Differences between IT and OT networks

Before continuing, let's highlight the differences between IT and OT networks.

The former manage a company's data, prioritizing the integrity and confidentiality of information. They typically connect:

- Servers
- Storage
- Office equipment
- Communication systems

OT networks favor an approach geared toward the stability and uptime of equipment, as well as the processes they control.

Both generate tangible benefits, but not without risks; industrial OT networks were originally designed to operate in isolation, without external connectivity, so most of these networks and infrastructures lack protection against external threats.

## The convergence of networks

Digital transformation in industry, driven by Industry 4.0, sensorization, and connectivity, is bringing OT systems and devices that were originally designed to operate in isolation onto the network. This evolution, while necessary to improve efficiency, productivity, and predictive maintenance, also entails a considerable increase in the attack surface. Systems that used to be disconnected are now accessible and require new protection strategies.

Physical and logical network segmentation thus becomes the first basic security measure for industrial environments. However, its implementation isn't simple, since there are numerous devices shared between IT and OT networks, which complicates their isolation. It's common to find, for example, industrial machine control equipment (such as HMIs or operator terminals) that only run on legacy operating systems, without support or the possibility of updates, making them especially vulnerable targets. These devices must be properly isolated, and it's essential to monitor all inbound and outbound traffic to and from them.

In this context, the right approach isn't to maintain total separation between IT and OT, but to move toward controlled convergence between both networks. This allows organizations to leverage the value of data generated by industrial devices to optimize processes, improve decision-making, and increase competitiveness. However, this integration must always be accompanied by specific protection mechanisms.

## Key components of an OT network

Below are the main elements that make up a modern OT architecture:

- **SCADA systems (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition):** Collect real-time data and enable supervision and control of distributed industrial processes.
- **DCS (Distributed Control Systems):** Manage and distribute control of complex, continuous industrial processes.
- **PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers) and sensors:** Automate and monitor specific physical processes with high reliability.
- **HMIs (Human-Machine Interfaces):** Interfaces that allow operators to interact with the processes controlled by the PLCs.
- **Industrial gateways:** Enable communication between OT devices and IT networks, acting as control and protocol translation points.

OT networks are characterized by their robust architecture and adaptability to the operating environment, using specific protocols such as Modbus, Profinet, or EtherNet/IP, which are very different from the standards typically used in IT networks.
